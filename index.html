04

DEMOCRACY

Imagine a group of animals choosing who will lead them. One candidate is powerful and frightening; another is gentle and familiar. The group is told that choosing is freedom, but nobody stops to ask whether the choices themselves were shaped long before the vote.

The deeper question is not simply, “Who should lead?” It is, “How do we decide what makes a leader worthy of trust?” A society can have a choice and still need wisdom about how that choice is made.

Democracy, in this philosophical sense, is more than selecting a name. It is the responsibility to question, to listen, to tolerate disagreement, and to remember that collective decisions have consequences beyond the moment of choosing.

“Freedom becomes meaningful when people are free to question the choices placed before them.”
